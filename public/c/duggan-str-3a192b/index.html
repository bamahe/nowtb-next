Key findingsWhat the data says

1. Average stay matters as much as legality. The cost seg strategy needs his average guest stay at 7 days or less. Market averages: Temple Terrace 5.2 nights, Tampa 5.3, Largo 6.3, Palm Harbor 6.5, Seminole 6.8, Siesta Key 6.8, Sarasota 7.1. Beach markets run long because of monthly snowbird stays, so any beach home needs a maximum-stay setting (for example 6 nights) to protect the average.

2. A pool roughly triples income in the beach markets. Seminole pool listings average $60,265 a year vs $21,833 without; Palm Harbor $46,816 vs $20,054. Every beach-area pick below has a pool.

3. Tampa proper is a weak fit for this strategy. Tampa has 4,388 active listings averaging $25,397 a year, mostly 1-bedroom units, and residential homes must rent 7+ nights. The only upscale Tampa-side home that is new, nightly-legal and flood zone X is in Temple Terrace (#4).

4. Inventory that meets every requirement is thin. I screened every active MLS listing in Tampa, Temple Terrace, Pinellas and Sarasota. Five homes clear all of his filters today. I will watch new listings daily and send anything that fits.

Before an offerWhat I verify on his top picks

  1. Property-level comps: AirDNA Rentalizer on each address: competing listings within 1 mile, their nightly rates, occupancy and 12-month revenue, and how saturated the immediate area is.
  2. Legality in writing: jurisdiction and zoning confirmation from the county or city, plus HOA documents where there is an HOA.
  3. Insurance quote for short-term rental use before the inspection period ends.
  4. Stay-length plan: a maximum-stay setting and booking calendar that keeps his average at 7 nights or less, reviewed with his CPA.
