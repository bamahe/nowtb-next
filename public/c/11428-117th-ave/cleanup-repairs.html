The short version

The house is structurally sound and the bones are fine. The kitchen was updated at some point and it still shows well. What you are paying for is not repair so much as reset: getting other people's belongings out, getting the walls and ceilings back to a neutral surface, and getting the air conditioning serviced.

Four things drive the cost, in this order:

  • The clean-out. Mattresses, bikes, tarps, yard debris, and a shed that needs to be emptied.
  • Smoke damage. The tenants smoked inside. Residue is through the ceilings and walls, worst around the supply vents, and the paint has let go under it in places. It drives both the scrubbing and the primer spec.
  • Interior paint, wall to wall. The walls are heavily soiled at chair-rail height throughout. This is not a touch-up job.
  • A/C service. The space above the air handler, filter included, is packed with black debris, and the data plate reads 2007.

Everything past those four is detail.

One thing worth confirming with the painter: smoke residue needs a stain-blocking primer, shellac or oil based. A standard latex primer will let the nicotine bleed back through the new paint within weeks, and then the whole job gets done twice.

One thing to settle before anyone orders

200 linear feet of quarter round looks short. Totalling the plank rooms one by one, the wall run comes to roughly 330 linear feet even after taking out door openings. Quarter round covers the expansion gap along every wall, and since the paint quote keeps and repaints the existing baseboard rather than replacing it, that gap needs covering everywhere the new floor meets a wall. My estimate could be off because I am working from video, but it is off by enough to be worth a tape measure before the order goes in. Running short here is a second trip, not a disaster.

The plank quantity checks out. With only the two bathrooms tiled, 54 cases covers about 1,307 sq ft against roughly 1,160 to 1,190 sq ft of plank area. That is a 10 to 12 percent overage, right at or a little above the 7 to 10 percent the trade allows for cuts and waste. No change needed.
