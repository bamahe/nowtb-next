The short version

The house is structurally sound and the bones are fine. The kitchen was updated at some point and it still shows well. What you are paying for is not repair so much as reset: getting other people's belongings out, getting the walls and ceilings back to a neutral surface, and getting the air conditioning serviced.

Four things drive the cost, in this order:

  • The clean-out. Mattresses, bikes, tarps, yard debris, and a shed that needs to be emptied.
  • The ceilings. Paint is peeling in sheets in several rooms, and the worst of it is directly around the air conditioning supply vents. That pattern points at the A/C, not the roof, but it needs a licensed eye before anyone paints over it.
  • Interior paint, wall to wall. The walls are heavily soiled at chair-rail height throughout. This is not a touch-up job.
  • A/C service. The air handler interior is packed with debris and the data plate reads 2007.

Everything past those four is detail.

Why the order matters

Paint peeling in a ring around supply registers is the classic signature of condensation, usually an uninsulated or leaking supply boot sweating into the ceiling in Florida humidity. It is normally a straightforward fix. But it can also be a roof leak, and the two look similar from below. I am not going to have anyone paint over this until an A/C contractor and a roofer have each put eyes on it. Painting first means paying twice and, worse, disclosing later.

I am also having the dark speckling assessed rather than assuming. It may be nothing more than surface mildew on failed paint, which is common and cheap to remediate. I would rather know, document it, and hand a buyer a resolved item than have it surface in their inspection.

Status: A/C evaluation and roof check being scheduled first. Repair quote follows once we know the cause. Nothing gets painted before then.
