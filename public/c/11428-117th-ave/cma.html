The short version

Your house sits in a pocket of Pinellas County where condition is doing almost all of the pricing work. Every comparable sale above $288 per square foot in the last six months was either fully renovated or had a pool. Every sale below that was dated.

That is good news, because it means the gap between what your house is worth today and what it is worth after a modest amount of work is unusually wide, and the work required is cosmetic rather than structural.

I pulled your permit history from Pinellas County, so two of the usual unknowns are already answered: the roof was replaced in 2015 and the sewer line in 2017. Both are documented below. The ranges assume average interior condition for a home of this age. I have not been inside yet, and once the tenants are out and I walk it I will tighten these numbers.

What the house has going for it

  • A bonus room with French doors off the living area. From the 2018 photos it reads as an enclosed sun room with windows on two walls and its own exterior door. I do not see a closet in any frame, so marketing it as a third bedroom is unlikely without adding one. It is still real, usable space, an office, a den, or a guest room, and most two bedroom homes at this price do not have it.
  • Quiet dead end street, under ten minutes to Indian Rocks Beach. Both are real selling points here and both go in the listing.
  • Two storage sheds, one wired with working outlets. A powered workshop shed is a genuine draw for a certain buyer, and it costs nothing to feature.
  • Parking for four. Carport plus driveway space for three more. That matters on a house with no garage.

Three things worth knowing

  • You are in FEMA Zone X, outside the high risk flood area. In Pinellas County right now that is a genuine advantage, and it is one of the first things buyers here check.
  • You are in a non evacuation zone and on county water and sewer, not a well or septic system. Combined with the flood zone above, that is a strong insurance and storm profile for a Pinellas listing right now.
  • The county lowered its 2026 preliminary value on the property to $258,753 from $289,312. That is the appraiser marking this pocket down, and buyers will have seen the same thing. It does not set your sale price, but it will come up.

Why the light make-ready is the right call

Your 2018 lease listing describes new flooring, fresh paint, new cabinets and new countertops done that summer, and the photos back that up. The interior is not 1958 original, it is eight years old with tenant wear on it. You are refreshing finishes rather than replacing them, which is a meaningful advantage.

Two honest caveats from those photos. The kitchen cabinets are dark and the counters are laminate, while the homes selling above $288 per square foot around you have quartz or stone. And the bathroom does not appear to have been part of that 2018 work. Neither needs a renovation to sell, but both are places where a buyer will push on price, so it is better to know now than to hear it in an inspection response.

The spread between selling dirty and selling clean in this neighborhood is wider than the cost of getting it clean. It is also the version of this that requires the fewest decisions from you, because it is a defined scope of work with a fixed quote rather than an open ended renovation. From the street the highest return items are exterior paint and the front lawn.

What the county permit records show

I pulled the permit history from Pinellas County. Two permits are on file for your property, and both were closed out properly.

April 2015 · Full roof replacement

Dimensional Tamko shingles, 25 squares, tear off and replace, permitted and finaled. Your roof is about eleven years old.

This is the most important fact in this report. In Florida, roof age decides whether a buyer can get an insurance policy, and insurance decides whether they can get a loan. At eleven years on a dimensional shingle roof you are comfortably inside what carriers will write, which keeps your house in the full retail buyer pool instead of limiting it to cash investors.

February 2017 · Sewer line replacement

Eighty feet of sewer line replaced, permitted and finaled.

For a 1958 house this matters more than it sounds. Original cast iron drain lines are a common and expensive failure in homes of this age in Pinellas County, and buyers’ inspectors look for it specifically. Yours was replaced and documented nine years ago. That goes in the listing.

What is not in the record

There is no permit on file for an air conditioner, water heater, electrical panel, or windows going back to at least 2001. Neighbors on your street have air conditioner permits on record from 2001 onward, so if yours had been replaced with a permit, it would appear.

Plan on the air conditioner being at or near the end of its life. A replacement runs roughly $6,000 to $9,000 installed. That is a budget line, not a threat to the sale, and it is the kind of thing we can address with a credit at closing rather than cash out of pocket. I will confirm the actual age from the data plate when I walk the property.
