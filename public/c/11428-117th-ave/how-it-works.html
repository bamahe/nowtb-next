Below is exactly how the three things you asked about actually work.

1. Signing from overseas

There are two routes. Which one fits depends on where each of you is and what your connectivity looks like.

Special Power of Attorney, the reliable route

Your base legal assistance office can usually draft one at no cost. It should be specific to this sale and name someone stateside you trust. Florida requires two witnesses plus a notary, and a military notary is valid overseas without a state commission, so fellow servicemembers can witness.

Remote online notarization, the convenient route

Florida permits signing by live video. It is genuinely easier when it works. It can also fail for people who have been overseas a while, because the identity check pulls from US credit and public records, and some platforms block foreign IP addresses outright. I would rather have a POA in hand as the backup than discover that three days before closing.

Handle it in one visit

If you go to legal assistance, get everything notarized at the same appointment: the power of attorney, and a short name affidavit. The county still shows title under the name used at the 2015 purchase, so your title company will likely want that affidavit. One trip instead of three scrambles across a time difference.

The listing agreement and the purchase contract are both e-signed. Nothing gets printed, mailed, or scanned.

2. The clean-out, repairs, and vendors

This starts the day the tenants hand over the keys, and it runs on a simple rule: I get quotes, you approve by email, you see photos before and after. You never wonder what is happening inside that house.

1
Documented walkthrough I walk the property and photograph and video everything before anything is touched or thrown away.
2
Secure the property Locks changed, keys accounted for, lockbox installed. Utilities stay on, because there are no photos, inspections, or appraisal without power and water.
3
Written quote before any spending Clean-out, haul-off, and repairs quoted in writing with photos of what the quote covers. You approve it before a dollar is spent.
4
Anything you want kept Tell me what matters and I will set it aside, photograph it, and arrange shipping or storage rather than guessing.
5
Make-ready and photography Work completed, after photos sent, then professional listing photos once it shows well.

I schedule and supervise the vendors. If something goes sideways, that is my problem to solve, not a phone call you have to make from another time zone.

One item with a deadline

Security deposit, 30 days from move-out

Florida gives you 30 days after the tenants vacate to send written notice by certified mail if you intend to claim any of their deposit. Miss that window and the full deposit goes back regardless. If neither of you can execute that from where you are inside 30 days, the cleaner move is usually to return it in full rather than lose the argument later. Your call, but the clock starts the day they leave.

What I need from you

  • A fifteen minute window for a call, any time zone.
  • Are you both deployed, and are you in the same location? It decides which signing route we take.
  • Is there someone stateside you trust to hold a power of attorney and take the keys?
  • The tenants’ confirmed move-out date, and a forwarding address if you have one.
  • A recent mortgage statement, or just the lender and current balance, so your net sheet shows a real payoff instead of an estimate.
