Why the repaint is not claimed, and what is

A repaint after a long tenancy is normal wear, and a tenant will say so. They would be right. This paint was eight years old and the house needed doing regardless of who lived in it, so the $3,144 repaint sits in the second table, not this one.

What the smoking added is a different question, and D&S have answered it on paper. The revised estimate carries two lines that a normal repaint does not: washing and degreasing every wall before primer at $1,000, and a shellac sealing primer upgrade at $350. Those exist only because paint will not bond to nicotine film. A vendor itemizing them separately is far stronger evidence than me arguing a whole repaint was smoke-driven.

That is the claim: the delta, not the job. It asks for less than the earlier version of this page did, and it is much harder to argue with.

Your two dates

The tenant moved out on September 3, 2026. Running both Florida clocks from that date:

Sep 18 If you are not claiming any of it. The full deposit has to be back with him within fifteen days. No notice needed, just return it.
Oct 3 If you are claiming any of it. Written notice has to be in the mail, certified, within thirty days, and it has to state what you are claiming and why. That is what the table above is for.
One wrinkle worth knowing

He had laundry still running in the machine on the 4th and his belongings are still outside, so a tenant could argue he had not fully given up possession on the 3rd and that both dates should sit later. That argument would help you, not hurt you. I have dated the clocks from the 3rd anyway, because working to the earliest defensible date is how you are never the one who filed late.

For completeness, the rest of the sequence:

then 15 Once the notice lands, he has fifteen days to object in writing. If he does not, you deduct the claim and send him the balance.

Miss the thirty day notice and the claim is gone, regardless of how good the documentation is. That is why this page exists and why it is dated.
