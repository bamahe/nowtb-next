What this means

  • FHA is cheaper today: about $5,900 less cash up front, about $112 a month less, a lower rate, and Highland's full closing cost package fits.
  • Conventional wins later, once the PMI comes off. It drops automatically when the loan reaches 78% of the original value, about 10.5 years in with regular payments. If values rise about 3% a year, you could request removal around year 5 or 6 with a new appraisal.
  • FHA mortgage insurance stays for the life of the loan. You can drop it by refinancing into a conventional loan later. If rates come down, that refinance can lower the rate and remove the mortgage insurance in one step.
  • Our recommendation: FHA on this home, unless you want to put 10% or more down. At 10% down, conventional allows up to 6% in builder help and the PMI is lower.
  • We're confirming with Highland's lender that closing cost help works the same way at Summerlake as at Bellaviva, and what the 4.99% "qualifying rate" on the FHA sheet means. Your real payment is at 5.49%.

About these numbers

  • Monthly estimates include principal and interest, FHA mortgage insurance, HOA, property taxes at about 1.45% of price. Homeowners insurance is included at an estimated $1,800 a year ($150 a month). FHA figures assume 3.5% down.
  • Adams 4.99% ARM payments are shown before any flex cash is applied. Using the flex cash for a buydown would lower them.
  • Highland's 5.49% rate is already bought down by Highland, which is why the 2.5% buydown concession on their website can't be added on top. On spec homes, included features like blinds can't be swapped for extra rate buydown.
  • We're comparing Highland's numbers against your own lender in the 30-day rate shopping window, so the credit checks don't hurt your score.
  • Seller credits on a resale: FHA allows up to 6% of the price. Conventional allows 3% with less than 10% down, and 6% with 10% or more down. We'll confirm the best loan type with your lender before we write the offer.
  • Rates, prices and incentives change often. Nothing here is a loan approval or a rate lock.
