What the comps changed

Two corrections before you read the numbers

The value is $337,000, and the deal lives or dies on management

The listing side is marketing a $350,000 value. Seven closings within a half mile in the last 90 days support $330,000 to $345,000, most probably $337,000. The seller's payoff is roughly $314,000, and after commission and costs they need about $337,000 to walk away with nothing. Their break even and the property's market value are the same number, so there is no discount hiding here.

Second, the operating number. Break even rent is $2,271 self managed and $2,523 with a 10% manager. The target rent on this house is $2,900, which clears break even by a wide margin either way and puts roughly $629 a month in your pocket self managed from day one.

The part that makes this worth doing

Price barely moves your cash flow

Because you inherit a fixed $1,514.96 payment, purchase price only touches the tax line. Bidding $325,000 versus $345,000 changes monthly cash flow by $31. It changes cash to close by $20,000, but not what the property carries.

That is the opposite of a normal purchase and it is your bidding advantage. You can go up to win without wrecking the economics. Protect the cash, not the cash flow.

The $250,000 is bait

It opened at $369,000 on April 1, stepped down through $364,000, $359,000, $349,000, back up to $354,000, went pending June 23, fell out June 26, then dropped to $250,000 on July 21 with a bid deadline attached. That is an auction structure, not an ask.

The sellers are trapped

Public records show a $348,096 VA loan taken December 27, 2021 at 3.25%. They bought at the peak with nothing down. The balance amortizes to about $314,000 today. Any price under roughly $337,000 requires them to bring cash to closing.

The contradiction we have to fix

You cannot assume a $314,000 loan on a $250,000 purchase. An assumption means you take over the full balance. If the accepted price falls below payoff, it stops being an assumption and becomes a short sale, and a short sale does not carry the 3.25% rate forward.

Civilian assumption is confirmed

The listing agent confirmed in writing that the VA and the sellers will allow a non veteran to assume. You still get underwritten by the servicer on credit, income and debt after the contract is signed. This is different from the Cambium Crown property, which requires veteran status.

The ceiling is not the rent

$3,800 is the maximum the authority will consider, not what they will approve. The binding constraint is the rent reasonableness test, where they compare this house against similar unassisted rentals nearby.

Target rent: $2,900

Live 3/2 comps in 33596 run from Shilo Ct at $2,150 up to Clareside Dr at $2,680. The subject sits at the top of that set on features: largest footprint at 1,558 sf, 2 car attached garage, 2024 roof, oversized fenced cul de sac lot and a screened lanai. Priced at $2,900 against a $3,800 voucher ceiling, there is no gap for the tenant to cover.

No gap risk

Because the payment standard sits so far above market, the voucher absorbs the full rent cleanly. The tenant is not stretching to cover a shortfall, which is the single biggest cause of voucher tenant default.

Straight talk

What could kill this deal

I would rather you walk in knowing these than find out at day 45.

RISK 01

The seller cannot afford your price

At $314,000 payoff plus commission and costs, the seller needs roughly $337,000 to net zero. Below that they bring cash. If they do not have it, the deal either dies or converts to a short sale that kills the 3.25% rate and runs six months.

RISK 02

It already fell out once

The listing went pending June 23 and collapsed June 26. Three days. That is either an inspection finding or a buyer who discovered the assumption math. I want to know which before we commit, and I will ask the listing agent directly.

RISK 03

Seller entitlement reversal

When a civilian assumes a VA loan, the seller's VA entitlement stays locked until that loan is paid off, potentially for 25 years. Sellers routinely agree and then back out once their lender explains it. We want that acknowledgment signed early.

RISK 04

Servicer timeline

Assumption approval runs 45 to 90 days and the servicer controls the pace. Any contract needs a realistic closing date and a contingency tied to assumption approval, not a standard 30 day close.

RISK 05

The tax reset

The seller's $4,742 bill reflects a homesteaded, capped assessment. On sale it resets to market with no exemptions. Budget $5,800 to $6,300. This alone swings cash flow by roughly $100 per month.

RISK 06

No reserves are modeled

Cash flow here is before maintenance and vacancy. Near term exposure is genuinely low with a 2024 roof and a 4 year old AC, but this is a 1992 house. Water heater, plumbing and the panel are the eventual line items, and a single vacancy month costs a full rent check.

How this runs

From here to keys

Eight steps. The first one has to happen before the August 4 deadline.

  1. Now, before we bid

    Pin down the payoff and the assumption terms

    I get the exact principal balance, escrow balance, servicer name, written confirmation that a civilian assumption is approved, and confirmation the seller can fund the shortfall at our price. No number goes into the portal until this is in hand.

  2. Before August 4

    Open at $325,000

    We submit through the seller's portal with proof of funds and standard contingencies intact, and we make the assumption structure explicit in the offer so it does not get treated as a cash bid.

  3. Days 1 to 3

    Contract and escrow

    Signed contract, deposit to Stewart Title, and the assumption package goes to the servicer immediately. This is the long pole, so it starts on day one.

  4. Days 3 to 12

    Inspection window

    Full inspection plus a four point. Roof is 2024 and the AC is roughly 4 years old, so the exposure is plumbing, electrical panel and 1992 era items. We also pre check NSPIRE items so the voucher inspection passes first pass.

  5. Days 10 to 60

    Servicer underwriting

    You get underwritten on credit, income and debt. Same documentation as a normal loan. The rate and payment do not change based on the outcome, you either qualify or you do not.

  6. Parallel track

    Line up the tenant

    We build the rent comp package for the housing authority and start marketing to voucher holders before closing, so the request for tenancy approval goes in the week we close instead of the month after.

  7. Closing

    Assumption funds

    You bring the gap above payoff plus the 0.5% funding fee and closing costs. The note transfers. Your payment is $1,514.96 and stays there for 25 years.

  8. Days 1 to 45 after close

    Inspection, HAP contract, first check

    Housing authority inspects, the HAP contract executes, and the authority's portion lands on a fixed date every month. Historically the most reliable rent check in the portfolio.
