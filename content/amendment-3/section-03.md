# ## What happens if you were not a Florida resident on December 31, 2026?

If voters approve Amendment 3 and you were not maintaining a permanent residence in Florida on December 31, 2026, you do not get the headline exemption when you buy. You get $25,000 against school taxes and up to $50,000 against everything else, and you wait until the fifth year of exemption to step up to the larger number. Anyone who was already a Florida resident on that date goes to up to $150,000 in 2027 and up to $250,000 in 2028.

The trigger sits in the constitutional text, not in a regulation someone can soften later. The reduced exemption applies to a person "who had not maintained a permanent residence in this state as of December 31, 2026." It is a one-time snapshot. Nothing you do in 2027 moves you across that line.

### What each group actually gets

| Tax year | Florida resident on Dec 31, 2026 | Not a Florida resident on Dec 31, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| 2027 | Up to $150,000 non-school | Up to $50,000 non-school |
| 2028 | Up to $250,000 non-school | Up to $50,000, CPI-adjusted starting Jan 1, 2028 |
| 2029 and after | $250,000 tier, CPI-adjusted starting Jan 1, 2029 | Still the $50,000 tier until the fifth year of exemption |
| Fifth year of exemption | Not applicable | Steps up to the $250,000 tier as CPI-adjusted |

The school column is the same for everybody, every year: $25,000, unchanged from current law. Amendment 3 does not touch the school portion of anyone's bill.

Against non-school millage, today's homestead exemption is $51,411 ($25,000 plus the CPI-adjusted $26,411 second band). A new resident's $50,000 lands just under that. For your first four years as a new Floridian, Amendment 3 is close to a wash against non-school millage, not a cut and not the windfall in the headline.

### Who this actually hits

- **Buyers relocating to Florida in 2027 or later.** Nearly every out-of-state buyer I work with next year lands here. Same house, same street, same millage as the neighbor, and a materially higher tax bill for four years.
- **Snowbirds converting a second home to a primary residence.** Owning Florida property is not the same as maintaining a permanent residence here. If your domicile is still New York or Ohio on December 31, 2026, the text reads against you even though you have held the Florida house for years. Confirm your exact standing with your county property appraiser before you assume anything.
- **Recent arrivals who have not nailed down permanent residence yet.** If you landed in 2026 and have not established permanent residence, the clock matters. That is a conversation to have now, not in March.
- **Not hit:** existing Florida residents who sell and buy again later in Florida. The test is residency on December 31, 2026, not the specific house. Portability is unchanged at a $500,000 maximum transferable Save Our Homes differential with the three-year lookback.

### What the four-year wait is worth

Pinellas County's property appraiser prices the full exemption using the 2025 countywide aggregate average non-school rate of 12.2011 mills, which produces about $1,203 of 2027 savings and $2,423 in 2028 for a homestead getting the full benefit.

Run the gap at that same rate. The $100,000 difference in exempt value in 2027 is roughly $1,220, and the $200,000 difference in 2028 is roughly $2,440. Both tiers then index for inflation on different schedules, the $250,000 tier beginning January 1, 2029 and the $50,000 tier beginning January 1, 2028. Four years of that is real money on a Hillsborough or Pinellas closing statement. Your actual number depends on your county and city millage, so treat these as scale, not a quote.

### The parts that are not settled

Three caveats.

First, the ballot summary voters will read says the increase begins "with the fifth year of exemption." The Legislature's enrolled joint resolution described the same rule as five years of Florida residency. The constitutional text keys entry into the reduced tier to residency on December 31, 2026, then runs the clock on years of exemption. The Pinellas property appraiser reads it as four years of homestead exemption, with the larger exemption starting January 1 of the fifth year. That conflict is live and part of the pending ballot-language litigation.

Second, the ballot summary hedges the whole rule with "to the extent permitted by the U.S. Constitution." That phrase limits the five-year wait to whatever federal law allows, which leaves it open to challenge.

Third, beginning January 1, 2030, a county or municipality may shorten the waiting period by a two-thirds vote of its governing body for a "critical local need." That is a local political decision, not a guarantee.

Nothing here is tax or legal advice. Verify your residency date and exemption status with your county property appraiser, and run the dollar impact past your CPA before you plan around it.
