# ## What Are Both Sides Saying About Amendment 3?

Amendment 3 reached the ballot out of the June 2026 special session, passing the House 75 to 26 and the Senate 30 to 9 as CS/HJR 1F. Because it is a joint resolution, the Governor had no veto to exercise. Here is each side at its strongest.

### The case for

**Who is making it:** Gov. Ron DeSantis, Senate President Ben Albritton, House Speaker Daniel Perez, sponsor Rep. Overdorf, companion sponsor Sen. Avila, and Sen. Don Gaetz.

- **The homeowner savings are large and automatic.** Against non-school millage, a homestead picks up roughly $98,589 in additional exempt value in 2027 and roughly $197,000 to $198,000 in 2028 compared to today's $51,411. The Pinellas County Property Appraiser puts that at about $1,203 in 2027 and $2,423 in 2028 at the 2025 countywide average non-school rate of 12.2011 mills. Homeowners who already have homestead do not file anything new.
- **It is not only a homeowner bill.** The non-homestead assessment cap drops from 10% to 5% for small rental properties of nine units or fewer and for commercial and second-home property, which supporters say reaches landlords and small businesses. DeSantis has rebutted the renters argument, calling it "a fundamental misunderstanding of what it actually does" and pointing to that lower cap.
- **Local budgets have grown.** Gaetz described the run-up in local government revenue as "the days of milk and honey," and the amendment constitutionally channels county and municipal property tax revenue into seven enumerated core needs including public safety, schools, infrastructure, natural resources, bond debt service, and employee retirement.
- **Let voters decide.** Albritton's special session memo framed it as a trust-the-voters question. Perez said he was "proud of the opportunity that we are giving the voters."
- **Existing protections stay in place.** Save Our Homes stays at 3% or CPI, whichever is lower, and portability stays at $500,000 with a three-year lookback.
- **It may pull buyers in, not push them out.** Shelton Weeks of the Lucas Institute for Real Estate and Finance at Florida Gulf Coast University says the relief could attract buyers and reverse outmigration driven by insurance and tax costs.

### The case against

**Who is making it:** the Florida Sheriffs Association, Florida State Fraternal Order of Police, Florida Professional Firefighters, Florida AFL-CIO, League of Women Voters, Florida Library Association, APA Florida, Sierra Club Florida, the Florida League of Cities, the Palm Beach County League of Cities, the Florida Association of Special Districts, Miami-Dade Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz, Jacksonville Mayor Donna Deegan, former Republican state Sen. Jeff Brandes, and Democratic gubernatorial nominee David Jolly.

- **The revenue hole is not small.** The Revenue Estimating Conference projects local non-school property tax losses of $4.95 billion in FY 2027-28, $8.78 billion in FY 2028-29, and a recurring $11.86 billion. No financial impact statement appears on the ballot, because legislatively referred amendments are not required to carry one.
- **"Public safety" is listed, not protected.** Brandes points out that the spending clause does not prioritize or guarantee public safety funding. It names eligible uses; it does not set floors.
- **The promised state backstop is not in writing.** DeSantis has pledged a backstop and targeted grants for fiscally constrained counties, but opponents note that no recurring state funding source appears in the enabling legislation.
- **The door to full elimination opens without another statewide vote.** The amendment requires the Legislature to prescribe a uniform procedure for counties and municipalities to raise the exemption up to full assessed value, and lets special districts do the same by referendum. The Florida Policy Institute reads that as a pathway to eliminating the non-school homestead property tax administratively.
- **The ballot language has already failed judicial review once.** Leon County Circuit Judge David Frank ruled on August 4, 2026 that the original title was "more akin to a political slogan" and "not fair or neutral," and that the summary "defectively omits at least one material fact." The Attorney General rewrote it. The summary voters will read says new residents must maintain residency for five years; the constitutional text says the step-up comes in the fifth year of exemption. Those are not the same test.
- **Costs may reappear elsewhere.** Homebuilders warn that the gap gets backfilled with impact fees, permit fees, mobility fees, and water and sewer connection fees. The Tax Foundation warns that income-restricted and affordable housing development "could decline as the capacity to raise rent is restricted."

### Head to head

| Question | Supporters say | Opponents say |
|---|---|---|
| Size of the cut | About $1,203 in 2027 and $2,423 in 2028 on a Pinellas homestead | Those savings are the same dollars as an $11.86 billion recurring local revenue loss |
| Police and fire | Public safety is the first enumerated use of property tax revenue | Listing a use is not funding it; sheriffs, FOP, and firefighters all oppose |
| Renters | The non-homestead cap drops from 10% to 5%, which reaches rental property | The 5% cap limits assessed value, not millage, and excludes school levies entirely |
| New residents | A five-year wait protects longtime Floridians | The U.S. Constitution hedge in the summary signals the drafters expect a federal challenge |
| Local control | Cities and counties can still approve other spending, and may raise the exemption further | A uniform state procedure to zero out non-school homestead tax removes local budget authority |
| Ballot language | The court-ordered rewrite fixed the problem | The corrected summary still conflicts with the operative constitutional text |

### What both sides concede

The 5% cap limits assessed value growth, not tax bills, because millage rates are set separately and can rise. The increased exemption does not touch non-ad valorem line items such as CDD, fire, solid waste, and stormwater assessments, which can be raised or newly adopted. School taxes do not change at all: the $25,000 school exemption is untouched, and non-homestead property stays uncapped for school millage. The Pinellas County Property Appraiser is clear that exact savings cannot be stated yet, because administration depends on implementing legislation the Legislature has not passed. Passage requires 60% of voters under Art. XI, s.5(e) of the Florida Constitution.
