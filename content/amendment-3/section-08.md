# ## What Do Florida Homeowners Ask Most About Amendment 3?

### Does the new exemption stack on top of the homestead exemption I already have?

No, it replaces the second exemption band instead of stacking on it. Today a Florida homestead gets $25,000 off every millage plus a CPI-adjusted $26,411 off non-school millage, $51,411 total ([Florida DOR](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/cpi_homestead_exemption.pdf)). Amendment 3 strikes that second band and substitutes up to $150,000 in 2027 and up to $250,000 in 2028 against non-school levies, so you end up with $25,000 against school taxes plus the new tier against everything else, not $51,411 plus $250,000 ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF), [Pinellas County Property Appraiser](https://www.pcpao.gov/sites/default/files/Proposed%202026%20Florida%20Property%20Tax%20Amendment%20FAQ_published.pdf)).

### How much would I actually save?

The real gain is the difference between the new exemption and the $51,411 you already get, applied only to the non-school part of your bill. That works out to about $98,589 of additional exempt value in 2027 and roughly $197,000 to $198,000 in 2028, because the $26,411 tier keeps getting CPI-adjusted in the meantime.

| Year | Non-school exemption | Net new exempt value | Pinellas estimate at 12.2011 mills |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 (current law) | $51,411 | n/a | n/a |
| 2027 | $150,000 | about $98,589 | about $1,203 |
| 2028 | $250,000 | about $197,000 to $198,000 | about $2,423 |

The dollar estimates come from the Pinellas County Property Appraiser, which ran $98,589 and $198,589 against the 2025 countywide aggregate average non-school rate of 12.2011 mills ([PCPAO FAQ](https://www.pcpao.gov/sites/default/files/Proposed%202026%20Florida%20Property%20Tax%20Amendment%20FAQ_published.pdf)). Your county's millage is different, so your savings will be different. Run your own number with your county property appraiser.

### Will Amendment 3 lower my school taxes?

No, Amendment 3 does not lower school taxes, because the school district homestead exemption stays at $25,000 and is untouched by this amendment. The enrolled text still reads "For school district levies, up to the assessed valuation of twenty-five thousand dollars" ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)). Every Florida homeowner keeps paying school millage on assessed value above $25,000, and on most Tampa Bay bills that is a large slice. One caution: the House staff analysis says the new exemption "would apply to all ad valorem taxes," which contradicts both the constitutional text and the ballot summary ([House analysis](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/Analyses/h0001z.SAC.PDF)). Trust the text.

### Do I have to apply or re-file for the bigger exemption?

No, if you already have homestead exemption on file the larger exemption applies automatically ([Hillsborough County Tax Collector](https://www.hillstaxfl.gov/property-tax-reform/)). If you do not have homestead on your primary residence, that is the filing you need to make with your county property appraiser, and it is the gate to everything else here.

### I just moved to Florida. When do I get the bigger exemption?

If you were not a permanent Florida resident as of December 31, 2026, you start with $25,000 against school levies and up to $50,000 against non-school levies, and you step up to the larger exemption beginning with the fifth year of exemption ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)). Pinellas reads it the same way: after four years of maintaining a Florida homestead exemption, you qualify for the larger one on January 1 of the fifth year ([PCPAO FAQ](https://www.pcpao.gov/sites/default/files/Proposed%202026%20Florida%20Property%20Tax%20Amendment%20FAQ_published.pdf)). One wrinkle: the ballot summary hedges the whole rule with "to the extent permitted by the U.S. Constitution" ([ballot summary](https://initiativepetitions.dos.fl.gov/InitiativeForms/FulltextPDF/10-110_AG.pdf)). Starting January 1, 2030, a county or city can shorten that waiting period by a two-thirds vote of its governing body for a "critical local need" ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)).

### Does Amendment 3 change Save Our Homes or portability?

No, Amendment 3 leaves both Save Our Homes and portability exactly as they are. The Save Our Homes cap on homestead assessment increases stays at 3% or the CPI change, whichever is lower, and Article VII section 4(d)(1) is untouched by the resolution ([House analysis](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/Analyses/h0001z.SAC.PDF)). Portability survives unchanged too, still a $500,000 maximum transferable differential with the same three-year lookback ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)).

### I own a rental or a second home. What does the 5% cap do for me?

Beginning January 1, 2027, the annual assessment increase cap on non-homestead property drops from 10% to 5% for all levies other than school district levies. It is written into two places in Article VII section 4: subsection (g) for residential property with nine units or fewer, and subsection (h) for everything else ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)). School millage is still assessed on full market value every year with no cap at all, and the Manatee County Property Appraiser points out that the change "only caps assessed value, not taxes," because millage rates are set separately and can rise ([Manatee PAO](https://www.manateepao.gov/data/downloads/Proposed%202026%20Florida%20Property%20Tax%20Amendment%20FAQ.pdf)).

### Will my CDD, fire, and stormwater charges go down too?

No, the increased homestead exemption applies to ad valorem taxes only and does not reduce non-ad valorem assessments such as CDD fees, solid waste, fire, or stormwater line items, and those can still be raised or newly adopted ([PCPAO FAQ](https://www.pcpao.gov/sites/default/files/Proposed%202026%20Florida%20Property%20Tax%20Amendment%20FAQ_published.pdf)). Plenty of Riverview and Wesley Chapel bills carry a CDD line, so pull your own bill and look at the two sections separately before you estimate anything.

### If it passes, when do I actually see it?

The amendment takes effect January 1, 2027, the change first shows up on the August 2027 TRIM notice, and it hits a real bill in November 2027 ([Florida Senate bill page](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/)). The $250,000 tier arrives a year later, on the 2028 roll, and from January 1, 2029 forward that figure is adjusted for inflation. The $50,000 new-resident tier starts its own CPI adjustment on January 1, 2028.

### What does it take to pass, and what is the fiscal tradeoff?

Amendment 3 needs at least 60% voter approval on November 3, 2026, and if it falls short current law stays exactly as it is ([House analysis](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/Analyses/h0001z.SAC.PDF)). The Revenue Estimating Conference projects the cut reduces local non-school property tax revenue by $4.95 billion in FY 2027 to 2028 and $8.78 billion in FY 2028 to 2029, with a recurring impact of $11.86 billion. The amendment also restricts county and city use of property tax revenue to seven categories: public safety, education and schools, infrastructure, natural resources, bond debt service, retirement benefits for employees, and operations and administration. Other spending can still be approved by county officers or the local governing body unless general law prohibits it. There is no financial impact statement on the ballot, because legislatively referred amendments are not required to carry one ([ballot](https://voteorangefl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/26GEN113-CB-EN-26-10-1-15-9-21.pdf)). Confirm your own numbers with your county property appraiser and your CPA before you make a buying, selling, or budgeting decision on this.
