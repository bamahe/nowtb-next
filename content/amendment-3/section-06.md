# ## What Does Amendment 3 Change for Buyers and Sellers Right Now?

Nothing changes on a contract you sign today. Amendment 3 has not passed. It needs approval from at least 60% of voters on November 3, 2026, it takes effect January 1, 2027 if it does pass, and it would not show up until the August 2027 TRIM notice and the tax bill delivered in November 2027 ([House staff analysis](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/Analyses/h0001z.SAC.PDF), [CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/)). One date inside it has real planning consequences, December 31, 2026, and it only matters to people who are not Florida residents yet.

| Your situation | What would change | What would not change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Already homesteaded in Tampa Bay | Non-school exemption goes to up to $150,000 in 2027 and up to $250,000 in 2028, replacing today's $51,411 structure | School taxes, Save Our Homes, portability, and non-ad valorem assessments like CDD, fire, and stormwater |
| Moving to Florida from out of state | You would get $25,000 school plus up to $50,000 non-school, with the larger exemption delayed | Your ability to buy, close, or homestead on the normal timeline |
| Buying a rental, second home, or non-homestead property | Annual assessed value growth cap drops from 10% to 5% on non-school levies | School levies stay uncapped at just value every year, and millage rates stay free to rise |
| Selling | Nothing about your proceeds or your Save Our Homes portability | Buyers will ask about it anyway, so have the real answer |

### Why December 31, 2026 is the only date that matters

The constitutional text draws the line at whether a person "had not maintained a permanent residence in this state as of December 31, 2026" ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)). Land on the wrong side of it and your homestead exemption is $25,000 against school levies plus up to $50,000 against everything else, instead of the $150,000 and then $250,000 non-school exemption existing residents get. That is a real gap, and it is the one thing an out-of-state buyer should ask about.

Here is why I still would not rearrange a relocation around it:

- It binds nothing unless 60% of voters approve it.
- The language describing the waiting period does not match itself. The rewritten ballot summary voters will see says the increase starts "beginning with the fifth year of exemption, to the extent permitted by the U.S. Constitution" ([Department of State](https://files.floridados.gov/media/711369/eng-2026-booklet-constitutional-amendpub-updated-20260903.pdf)). The summary carried in the enrolled joint resolution framed it as five years of Florida residency ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)). Years of residency and years of exemption are two different clocks, and that conflict is unresolved.
- Ballot language here has already been litigated once. On August 4, 2026, Leon County Circuit Judge David Frank ruled the original title and summary defective, called the title "more akin to a political slogan" that "is not fair or neutral," and ordered the Attorney General to submit corrected language within 10 days ([Division of Elections](https://constitutionalinitiatives.dos.fl.gov/)).
- The delayed exemption for new residents applies only "to the extent permitted by the U.S. Constitution," a condition written into the ballot summary itself ([Department of State](https://files.floridados.gov/media/711369/eng-2026-booklet-constitutional-amendpub-updated-20260903.pdf)).
- Beginning January 1, 2030, a county or municipality can shorten the wait by a two-thirds vote of its governing body for a "critical local need" ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)).

Establishing Florida permanent residence is its own legal test, and it is not the same thing as closing on a house in December. Confirm what qualifies, and by when, with your county property appraiser before you move a closing date for tax reasons.

### Sellers: your numbers run the same way they always did

Save Our Homes is untouched. The 3% or CPI cap, whichever is lower, survives intact, and portability keeps its $500,000 maximum transferable differential and its three-year lookback ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)). A move-up or move-down seller runs the exact same math this year as last year.

What changes is the conversation. Buyers are hearing savings figures. The Pinellas County Property Appraiser estimates roughly $1,203 in 2027 and $2,423 in 2028 for a homestead receiving the full exemption, built on the 2025 countywide aggregate average non-school rate of 12.2011 mills ([PCPAO](https://www.pcpao.gov/sites/default/files/Proposed%202026%20Florida%20Property%20Tax%20Amendment%20FAQ_published.pdf)). That is one county, one millage assumption, and a projection, and it applies to the non-school portion of the bill only. It does not belong in a listing description.

### Investors and second home buyers

The non-homestead cap drops from 10% to 5% in two places, covering residential property of nine units or fewer and all other non-homestead real property, and both apply only to levies other than school district levies ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)). For school millage, non-homestead property stays completely uncapped and gets assessed at just value every year. The cap also limits assessed value growth, not the tax bill. Millage rates are set separately by each taxing authority and can rise, so a capped assessment does not guarantee a capped bill.

### What I have clients verify before they sign

1. **The school portion of the current tax bill.** It does not move. The $25,000 school exemption is unchanged, so that line stays where it is ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)).
2. **Every non-ad valorem line.** CDD, fire, solid waste, stormwater. The homestead exemption applies to ad valorem millage, so it does not reduce any of those assessments ([enrolled CS/HJR 1F](https://www.flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/BillText/er/PDF)).
3. **Whether homestead is already on file** with the county property appraiser, and what the exemption status will be after closing.
4. **New construction pricing.** The Revenue Estimating Conference projects local non-school property tax revenue down $4.95 billion in fiscal year 2027 to 2028 and $8.78 billion in fiscal year 2028 to 2029, with a recurring impact of $11.86 billion ([House staff analysis](https://flsenate.gov/Session/Bill/2026F/1F/Analyses/h0001z.SAC.PDF)). Ask any builder how that is reflected in their fee schedule.
5. **The millage assumption behind any savings number** somebody quotes you.

### Why nobody should restructure a purchase around an amendment that has not passed

Nobody can hand you an exact number today. The ballot summary itself requires the Legislature to prescribe a uniform procedure for counties and municipalities, and that implementing law does not exist yet ([ballot summary](https://voteorangefl.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/26GEN113-CB-EN-26-10-1-15-9-21.pdf)). Underwrite the house to the tax bill that exists right now. If voters approve it, your November 2027 bill gets better and you keep the difference. If they do not, you still own a property that penciled on day one. Run the actual numbers past your CPA or your county property appraiser before you treat any of this as a budget line.
