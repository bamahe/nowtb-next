# ## What Would Amendment 3 Actually Save You?

The biggest misunderstanding I hear about Amendment 3: **an exemption cuts your taxable value, not your tax bill.** A $150,000 exemption does not take $150,000 off what you owe. It takes $150,000 off the number your millage rate gets multiplied against. One mill equals $1 of tax per $1,000 of taxable value, so $150,000 of exempt value at Pinellas County's 12.2011-mill average non-school rate is worth roughly $1,830, not $150,000.

### Start With the Right Baseline

Amendment 3 does not stack on your current exemption. It replaces the second band. Here is the before and after:

| | Against school millage | Against all other millage (county, city, special district) |
|---|---|---|
| **Today (2026)** | $25,000 | $51,411 total ($25,000 plus the CPI-adjusted $26,411) |
| **2027, if it passes** | $25,000 (unchanged) | up to $150,000 |
| **2028, if it passes** | $25,000 (unchanged) | up to $250,000 |

So the new exempt value you actually gain against non-school millage is about **$98,589 in 2027** and about **$198,589 in 2028**, not the headline $150,000 and $250,000. Against school millage, the change is zero. School taxes stay on your bill exactly as they are now.

One refinement on the 2028 figure: it assumes the current $26,411 tier stays frozen at its 2026 value. That tier is CPI-indexed, so the true 2028 gain lands closer to $197,000 to $198,000.

### Real Numbers at Real Home Values

The Pinellas County Property Appraiser published the cleanest public math on this, using the 2025 countywide aggregate average non-school rate of **12.2011 mills**. I ran that same rate across a few assessed values. These are the non-school portion only.

| Assessed value | New exempt value, 2027 | Est. 2027 savings | New exempt value, 2028 | Est. 2028 savings |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $120,000 | $68,589 | about $837 | $68,589 | about $837 |
| $200,000 | $98,589 | about $1,203 | $148,589 | about $1,813 |
| $300,000 | $98,589 | about $1,203 | $198,589 | about $2,423 |
| $500,000 | $98,589 | about $1,203 | $198,589 | about $2,423 |

Three things the table shows:

1. **The 2027 benefit is flat** for anyone assessed at or above $150,000. Everybody in that group gets the same $98,589 of new exempt value and the same dollar savings. A $500,000 home does not save more than a $300,000 home.
2. **Lower assessed homes hit the ceiling.** You cannot exempt value you do not have. At $120,000 assessed, the 2027 exemption already wipes out your entire non-school taxable value, so 2028 adds nothing further.
3. **The 2028 step up is where the real money is**, and only homes assessed at or above $250,000 capture the full $198,589.

### Three Caveats Before You Spend It

**That is assessed value, not market value.** If you have held your homestead for years, Save Our Homes has kept your assessed value well below what the house would sell for. Amendment 3 does not change Save Our Homes. Pull your actual assessed value off your TRIM notice, not your Zillow estimate.

**Your millage is not Pinellas' millage.** 12.2011 mills is one county's 2025 average. Your county, city, and special district rates are different, and taxing authorities set them every year. Nothing in Amendment 3 stops a millage rate from going up. A bigger exemption against a higher rate can net out smaller than the table suggests.

**Non-ad valorem lines do not move.** CDD fees, fire assessments, solid waste, and stormwater are flat charges, not value-based taxes. The exemption does not touch them, and they can be raised or newly adopted.

### When You Would Feel It

Nothing changes on your 2026 bill. If voters approve it, the amendment takes effect January 1, 2027, first appears on your **August 2027 TRIM notice**, and first shows up on a **tax bill delivered in November 2027**.

One honest limit on all of this: the amendment itself requires the Legislature to prescribe a uniform procedure for counties and municipalities to administer and further raise the exemption, and that procedure does not exist yet. Run your own figures off your assessed value and your own non-school millage, then confirm them with your county property appraiser or your CPA before you plan around the savings.
